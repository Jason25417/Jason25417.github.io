---
title: A navigation set for learning a field top-down
description: How I pick which concepts to learn first when a field is new to me.
date: 2026-11-16
---

When a field is new to me, I do not start from its first chapter. I start by collecting a small set of its top-level or frontier concepts, and I call that collection a navigation set.

## How the set is chosen

Every concept rests on others: its definition uses terms that have their own definitions, and so on down. Call that the concept's dependency subgraph.

I choose the set under two pressures:

- **Wide coverage.** Together, the subgraphs should reach as much of the field as possible.
- **Little overlap.** Two concepts that rest on the same foundations teach me those foundations twice. I keep one of them.

This is a set-cover problem in spirit. I do not solve it exactly. A rough pass is enough to stop me from picking five concepts that are all the same idea in different clothes.

## Why top-down

Starting from the top gives every definition a reason to exist. When I walk down from a concept I already want to understand, each prerequisite arrives as the answer to a question I am already asking.

It also tells me when to stop. A prerequisite that no concept in the set depends on can wait.
